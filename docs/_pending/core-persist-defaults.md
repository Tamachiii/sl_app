Add after the last bullet `core-draft-unsynced` adds to this section (it starts "**`my-draft` and `draft-tree` are kept in the cache for 14 days"):

- **What the device has read is kept for 14 days, and one constant says so in three places.** `PERSIST_MAX_AGE_MS` in [`lib/queryClient.js`](../src/lib/queryClient.js) is the `maxAge` of the persisted cache (`App.jsx`), the `gcTime` of every root of `PERSISTED_QUERY_ROOTS` (one `setQueryDefaults` call per root — a second call for the same root REPLACES the first, so a root's refetch rules go into `REFETCH_RULES` and are merged there), and the age at which `shouldPersistQuery` stops storing one entry. They have to agree: an entry nobody observes is collected after its `gcTime`, and what leaves memory is written out of IndexedDB on the next save. With React Query's default of five minutes a session opened at home was off the device before the gym, and `my-confirmed-session-ids` — observed by the training list only — was gone five minutes into any workout, which made the list start again from the first session at the next launch without a network. A root added to the allow-list gets the `gcTime` by being in the list.
- **`shouldPersistQuery` is also the only thing that takes an entry OFF the device.** With a 14-day `gcTime` nothing is collected in an app that is opened every day (the timers start again at each launch), so an entry left out of a save is what "evicted" means: it is not there after the next launch, while the running app keeps it in memory. Two rules: an entry whose `dataUpdatedAt` is older than `PERSIST_MAX_AGE_MS`, and every entry of a session confirmed more than `CONFIRMED_SESSION_KEPT_MS` (7 days) ago — `session`, `set-logs`, `slot-comments`, `slot-deviations`, `session-confirmation`, `set-videos` and `last-performance`, whose keys carry the session id at the position `SESSION_ID_AT` gives. "Confirmed … ago" is read off the `confirmed_at` of the `['session-confirmation', sessionId]` entry in the cache; a session whose confirmation is not cached, or was undone (`null`), is not known to be finished and stays. A root that is keyed by session and joins the allow-list joins `SESSION_ID_AT`, or its entries of finished sessions pile up in a blob that is re-serialised on every change of the cache. One exception comes before both rules: a draft tree that carries the unsynced marker, and the `my-draft` pointer to it (`holdsUnsyncedDraft`), are stored whatever their age. What the rules cannot touch is the queue: a write that waits is a mutation, stored apart from the queries, so a session whose entries were left out still gets its queued sets and its confirmation sent. The function is also called by other suites with a bare `{ queryKey, state: { status: 'success' } }` to ask "is this key stored": without `data` and `dataUpdatedAt` it answers on the key alone, and has to keep doing so.
- **An entry that holds data is stored whatever became of its last fetch, and is stored as what was read.** A refresh that fails keeps the data and sets `status: 'error'`; `shouldPersistQuery` used to store `'success'` only, so one request lost in a basement took the session off the device. It now stores anything with data (a read that never succeeded has none). React Query restores a status as it finds it, and without a network an entry restored as `'error'` stays one — so `serializeCache`, the persister's `serialize`, rewrites such an entry (`asLastRead`: `status: 'success'`, `error` and the failure counters cleared) and keeps `isInvalidated`, which a failed fetch sets: the entry comes back as data that is out of date, and is read again as soon as it can be. A page may therefore treat `isError` on a persisted root as "this launch could not read it"; it never means "the stored copy is broken".
- **A return to the app re-reads five roots only when they are due — and the rule is not the same for all five.** Left to the client's defaults (stale after two minutes, refetch on focus and on reconnect) every unlock after a rest re-read the whole logging page, the athlete's entire set history included. `REFETCH_RULES` in `lib/queryClient.js` takes `session`, `slot-comments`, `slot-deviations`, `exercise-library` and `last-performance` out of that, on the strength of the invalidations the app's own writes already make. Rule 1, the first four (`QUIET_WHILE_OPEN`): `refetchOnWindowFocus` and `refetchOnReconnect` are the function `dueOnReturn` — true only for an entry the app flagged (`isInvalidated`: an invalidation that could not be served, a refresh that failed) or one older than `QUIET_MS` (4 hours, longer than any workout); their `staleTime` is NOT raised, so a page that opens on a copy older than two minutes still reads it again. Rule 2, the athlete's own hint (`['last-performance', 'self', …]`): `staleTime` is `QUIET_MS` (`hintStaleTime`), opening the page included; the hint a coach reads, keyed by the athlete, keeps two minutes. **Do not "simplify" rule 1 into a long `staleTime`.** These four keys are shared by both roles and written from another device — the coach edits a session the athlete holds, the athlete's notes and swaps are what the coach's review shows, the coach adds an exercise on a second device — nothing pushes those edits, and the cache is stored with its timestamps, so relaunching the app does not make a copy older: with a long `staleTime` an athlete would train on the session as it was before the coach's edit, with no way to get the new one. What rule 1 costs is the refresh a return to a page used to give: a coach's editor or review left open is brought up to date by reopening it (or by its own next write, which invalidates), not by coming back to the tab, until four hours have passed. Two things silently undo the rules: an option set in the hook (`useQuery({ staleTime })` wins over a root default — `useLastPerformance` sets none for that reason), and a read of one of these roots that relies on focus to stay current — `SessionView` asks for the session row explicitly at the moments a review may have happened for that reason (the bullet "**`reviewed_at` comes from a cached row, and nothing pushes a review to the athlete's device.**"). The request counts are in `src/lib/queryClient.cache.test.js` ("the phone is unlocked after a rest").

## docs/INVARIANTS.md — Coach Athletes-roster layout

Replace the bullet that starts "**Nothing invalidates `['last-performance']`: a hint is renewed by its own `staleTime` (one minute) and by nothing else.**" with:

- **An athlete's own "Last time" is held as fresh for four hours, and a confirmation is what renews it.** `hintStaleTime` in [`lib/queryClient.js`](../src/lib/queryClient.js) gives `['last-performance', 'self', …]` a `staleTime` of `QUIET_MS`: deriving a hint is the read of the whole done-set history once that list is older than `HISTORY_STALE_MS`, and the logging page is returned to twenty times a workout. On the athlete's device the hints of the OTHER sessions change when a session is confirmed or un-confirmed, so `refreshAfter` in [`lib/offlineMutations.js`](../src/lib/offlineMutations.js) calls `flagLastTimeHints` for those two writes: every cached hint but the session's own (a hint never looks at its own session) is invalidated with `refetchType: 'none'` — flagged, not read. A confirmation therefore still costs no history read, which `src/hooks/sharedHistory.test.jsx` pins ("the confirmation reads nothing"); a flagged hint is derived when its session is opened, or on the next return to a page that shows it, and the flag is stored with the entry, so across relaunches too. Three consequences. (1) `refreshAfter` is the `onSettled` DEFAULT of the confirm and undo keys: a hook that sets its own `onSettled` on those keys replaces it, and must flag the hints itself — `invalidateConfirmationQueries` in `hooks/useSessionConfirmation.js` does, for as long as those hooks carry a list of their own — or the next session's hint shows the workout before the one just trained for up to four hours. `src/lib/queryClient.cache.test.js` confirms and undoes through the real hooks and fails when neither path flags. (2) What is not covered is training that was never confirmed: sets ticked in session A do not renew the hint of session B, opened in the previous four hours, until A is confirmed or the four hours are over — do not close that by invalidating on every set tap, which is a history read per tap. (3) A coach's hint (the key names the athlete's `students` row instead of `'self'`) keeps the client's two minutes: it moves when the athlete trains, on another device, and nothing on the coach's device says so.

## docs/INVARIANTS.md — Web Push (rest-timer "Rest done" lock-screen notification)

Add after the bullet that starts "**iOS support requires the PWA be installed.**":

- **The device's subscription is written to the database at most once an hour, except when it has to be.** [`usePushAutoHeal`](../src/hooks/usePushAutoHeal.js) looks on load and on every return to the tab, as before, but each look used to be an upsert of `push_subscriptions` — one per unlock of a workout. It now writes when the last write for this user is older than `HEAL_EVERY_MS`, and at once in two cases the hour must not delay: another account on this device (the stamp names the user), and an endpoint that is no longer the one last written — the worker re-subscribes on `pushsubscriptionchange` but only the page knows who is signed in, and until the row has the new address every push to this device fails. The stamp is `localStorage['sl_push_heal']` = `{ userId, endpoint, at, ok }`, stored and not kept in memory because iOS closes an installed app behind the lock screen, which would make "once on load" once per unlock again. A write that failed is noted with `ok: false` and tried again after `HEAL_RETRY_MS` (5 minutes), not at every unlock; a device that is offline tries nothing and notes nothing; `reconcilePushSubscription` answering `false` (notifications off here) sent nothing and notes nothing. The comparison of endpoints reads the browser (`getActivePushSubscription`), never the database.

Add after the paragraph that starts "A query may derive from another one instead of reading the network:":

The client's defaults — stale after two minutes, refetch on mount, on focus and on reconnect when stale, collected five minutes after the last observer — are changed per ROOT in `src/lib/queryClient.js`, never per hook, for the twelve roots that are persisted. All twelve get `gcTime: PERSIST_MAX_AGE_MS` (14 days, the `maxAge` of the persisted cache). Five get a refetch rule from `REFETCH_RULES`: `session`, `slot-comments`, `slot-deviations` and `exercise-library` are read again when a page opens on a copy older than two minutes but not on a return to the app or to the network before `QUIET_MS` (4 hours), unless the entry was flagged; `['last-performance', 'self', …]` has a `staleTime` of `QUIET_MS`. A hook that reads one of these roots sets no `staleTime`, `gcTime` or `refetchOn…` of its own: an option passed to `useQuery` replaces the root's default.

## README.md — Offline Support (Student Session Logging)

Replace the bullet that starts "- **Read cache**" with:

- **Read cache** — what the student needs offline (the session and its sets, notes, swaps, videos and confirmation, the active block, the list of confirmed sessions, the "Last time" hints, the exercise library, a draft being written) is persisted to IndexedDB via `@tanstack/react-query-persist-client` + `@tanstack/query-async-storage-persister` (storage shim built on `idb-keyval`). On reload while offline, the cached state hydrates and the app is fully readable. An entry is kept for 14 days after it was last read, in memory and on the device alike — a session opened at home is still there at the gym — and it is kept when a later refresh fails. The entries of a session are dropped from the device a week after its confirmation. The allow-list (`PERSISTED_QUERY_ROOTS`) and the rules live in [src/lib/queryClient.js](src/lib/queryClient.js).
- **Fewer requests during a workout** — unlocking the phone after a rest does not re-read the session, its notes and swaps, the exercise library or the "Last time" history: they are read when the page opens, refreshed by the app's own writes, and left alone on a return to the app for four hours. Only the sets and the confirmation are re-read on unlock.
