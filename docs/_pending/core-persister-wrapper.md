Add after the last bullet `core-persist-defaults` adds to this section (it starts "**A return to the app re-reads five roots only when they are due"):

- **The device stores the writes of the queue — every one that has not settled — and no other mutation.** `isQueuedWrite` in [`lib/persistence.js`](../src/lib/persistence.js) is `state.status === 'pending'` and a `mutationKey` that is one of `MUTATION_KEYS`, part for part (`isQueueKey`); it is the `shouldDehydrateMutation` of `persistOptions`. Both halves replace the library's default, which stores whatever is *paused*. (1) **The key.** A mutation restored from IndexedDB is a key and its variables; its function comes from the defaults registered under that key. One stored without a key of the queue comes back with no function, fails with "No mutationFn found" and shows "Couldn't save that" — to an athlete who had only opened a session without signal, whose page paused a housekeeping write. (2) **Not settled, rather than paused.** A write is paused while the device is offline. On a network that does not answer it is not: its request is out for as long as the phone lets it hang, or it sleeps between two attempts, and while nobody is signed in it sleeps and looks again without limit. An app killed in one of those moments used to lose the write. Storing a write whose request may have reached the database is safe for one reason only: every function of `MUTATION_FNS` can be sent twice — which [`src/lib/offlineSafety.test.js`](../src/lib/offlineSafety.test.js) reads off the code (`WHAT_A_QUEUED_WRITE_SENDS`: an `update` of the row named by the variables, an `upsert` with `onConflict`, a `delete`, or the `save_draft_tree` RPC; never an `insert`). **A new queued write** therefore needs four things, not three: the key in `MUTATION_KEYS`, the function in `MUTATION_FNS`, its line in `WHAT_A_QUEUED_WRITE_SENDS`, and its key in the list pinned by `src/lib/persistence.test.js`.
- **A write is stored, and taken back, as one that waits.** `asWaiting` in `lib/persistence.js` runs on the way to the store (`persistClient`) and on the way back (`restoreClient`): `isPaused: true`, the failure count and its reason reset, and no `context`. `isPaused` is what `resumePausedMutations` looks for — a write restored as "pending, not paused" is never resumed and, being the first pending mutation of its scope, blocks every write behind it for good. `context` is the snapshot a hook took for its rollback (every cached list of sets, once per queued set): a restored write has no hook around it, so nothing can read it, and it was most of the blob the persister re-writes at each change of the cache. The live mutation is not touched — its hook still rolls back from its own `context`. Consequence for the mutation defaults in `lib/offlineMutations.js`: a handler registered there must not read `context` (the third argument of `onSuccess` / `onError` / `onSettled`); after a relaunch it is `undefined`. The restore also drops every stored mutation that is not a pending write of the queue, so a blob written by a build that stored more (the one published on 2026-10-13 stores every paused mutation) gives back only what can be replayed.
- **`CACHE_BUSTER` is the version of the stored READS. The queue goes through a change of it, and through the 14 days.** `persistQueryClientRestore` throws the whole blob away when its `buster` differs or it is older than `maxAge` — the mutation queue with it: the one documented way to handle a change of shape would have deleted the unsynced workout of every device it reached, and so did two weeks without opening the app. So `restoreClient` in `lib/persistence.js` makes both tests itself, on the reads alone, and hands the library a blob that always passes its own (current `buster`, fresh `timestamp`): a blob of another version, too old or without a date gives back **no read and every queued write**. Two kinds of read are kept with the queue, because no request can make them again: a `['draft-tree', id]` entry that carries the unsynced marker, and the `['my-draft', …]` entry that points to it. Three rules follow. (1) Change `CACHE_BUSTER` (in `lib/persistence.js`, nowhere else) when the shape of a persisted query changes in a way the pages cannot read; it costs every device one refetch and nothing else. (2) **A key of `MUTATION_KEYS` is never removed or renamed**: a device may hold a write made under it by any earlier build, the restore drops what has no registered key, and that is a workout thrown away without a word. `src/lib/persistence.test.js` pins the eleven keys. (3) **A write whose VARIABLES change shape gets a new key next to the old one**, and the old key keeps its old function for as long as a device may still hold a write made under it — the buster does not version the queue, and an old-shaped write is replayed by the function of today (a confirmation queued before `performedOn` existed is sent without its training date).
- **The persisted blob says whose it is, and a blob left by another account is wiped at launch.** The persister of `lib/persistence.js` writes `owner` (a user id, or `null`) next to `buster` and `timestamp`, reads it back at launch (`queryPersister.getOwner()`), and forgets it in `removeClient()`. A state is labelled with the owner of the moment it was TAKEN, not of the moment the throttled write lands — so what one account's late write leaves after a wipe is still under that account's name. `setOwner(userId)` only labels (and writes the label at once, without waiting for another change of the cache); it never relabels another account's data, which is why `ClearOnUserChange` in [`App.jsx`](../src/App.jsx) wipes first. That component wipes memory (`queryClient.clear()`) and device (`removeClient()`) in two cases: the signed-in user id changed while the app was open — any change, a sign-out included —, or the restored blob names an owner that is not the signed-in user. It decides nothing while auth is loading, and nothing while the blob is still being read (`useIsRestoring()`): a wipe then would be followed by the restore it was meant to prevent, so a change seen in that moment is remembered and acted on once the restore is over. It labels the cache only with a KNOWN account: at a launch where nobody is signed in — which is also what a token that could not be refreshed looks like — the cache stays its owner's. A blob without `owner` (written before the blobs said) is kept, as before, and labelled with the account that opens it. `src/App.test.jsx` holds the cases on the real client and persister.
- **A queued write is sent under the session of the account its queue belongs to, and under no other.** The wipe above comes late for one thing: the provider replays the restored queue from its `onSuccess`, before any component can look at the owner, and a write asleep between two attempts wakes up whenever its delay is over. Sent under another account's session such a write is refused by RLS — taken off the queue, and reported to the wrong person. So `lib/queryClient.js` registers the queue with `registerOfflineMutationDefaults(queryClient, { queueOwner })`, where `queueOwner` reads `queryPersister.getOwner()`, and `ownsQueue` in [`lib/offlineMutations.js`](../src/lib/offlineMutations.js) is asked in the two places that already ask for a session: `resumeMutationsAndReconcileDrafts` replays and reconciles nothing, and `queuedWrite` throws `AuthNotReadyError` — the write waits, exactly as it does while nobody is signed in — until the wipe removes it or its owner is back. An owner that is not known (`null`: nothing restored, a blob written before the label, a client registered without `queueOwner`) holds nothing back.
- **The cache is saved once at the moment the provider starts saving, by the LAST child of the provider.** `PersistQueryClientProvider` subscribes to the caches only when the restore is over — and the restore includes the promise of `onSuccess`, the replay of the queue, which is waited for while it moves (seconds on a slow network, with the restored pages on screen). What changes before that is in memory only: a queued write that reached the database is still stored as owed (and is sent a second time if the app is killed), and a set ticked meanwhile is not stored at all, until something else happens to change the cache. `SaveWhenRestored` in `App.jsx` calls `persistQueryClientSave` when `useIsRestoring()` turns false. Its position is load-bearing: React runs the effects of a commit children first and siblings in order, so as the last child of the provider its effect runs after those of every page and of `ClearOnUserChange` (a wipe decided in that pass is saved as the emptied cache, not followed by a save of what was wiped; a write a page makes as it mounts is in the save) and immediately before the provider's own, which subscribes — nothing can happen in between. `CacheBoundary` passes `persistOptions` whole; rebuilding it option by option in `App.jsx` is how the persister and the filter that goes with it come apart.
- **After a relaunch each scope replays in the order its writes were made — tested through the store.** `src/lib/persistence.test.js` drives the real hooks without a network, saves with the app's `persistOptions` (the library's `persistQueryClientSave`), reads the string in the store, restores it into a freshly evaluated app (`persistQueryClientRestore`) and looks at the requests in the order they leave: two taps on one set stay in order, the sets of two rows do not wait for each other, confirm then undo then a note leave one by one, the snapshots of a draft stay in order on a scope that holds no set back, and a set is dated at the tap. A test that hands `dehydrate()` output straight to `hydrate()` skips the three places where this goes wrong — the filter, the stored shape and the restore rules.

## docs/INVARIANTS.md — Messaging / notifications

Replace, in the bullet that starts "**A student-surface write is either offline-keyed or explicitly gated", the last sentence ("Every `ONLINE_ONLY` entry is a promise that the UI disables the affordance offline; keep that promise.") with:

Every `ONLINE_ONLY` entry is a promise that the write does not START without a network, and the same file checks it: the hook reads the state of the network itself, or every source file that calls the hook does — `useOnlineStatus()`, `onlineManager.isOnline()` or `navigator.onLine`. Without the guard such a write does not fail offline: it pauses, runs whenever the network returns on whatever the page held at the time, and — not being part of the queue — is gone if the app is closed first. The check is per file, so it cannot tell that the value is used on the right line; what it catches is an entry added with nothing near it looking at the network. One entry is excused by name, `useEnsureSetLogs` in `LEGACY_UNGATED`, and only for as long as the hook does not call the `ensure_set_logs` RPC. ## README.md — Offline Support (Student Session Logging)

Replace, in the bullet that starts "- **UX**", the two sentences from "The count is **derived**, never mirrored into local state:" up to and including "would otherwise flash the banner." with:

The count is the number of writes this device still owes the database — `isQueuedWrite` in [src/lib/persistence.js](src/lib/persistence.js), the same test that decides what is stored for the next launch — so a page that merely opened offline is not announced as "1 change waiting to sync". It is **derived**, never mirrored into local state: `useIsMutating` is a `useSyncExternalStore` read over the mutation cache, so the banner can never `setState` in the middle of another component's render.

Replace the bullet that starts "- **Per-user scoping**" with:

- **Per-user scoping** — the persisted blob records the account it belongs to. `ClearOnUserChange` in [App.jsx](src/App.jsx) wipes both the in-memory queryClient and the IndexedDB blob whenever the signed-in user changes (logout / login / switch), and at launch when the blob was left by another account; until then a queued write is not sent under another account's session.
- **What survives an update, a long pause and a kill** — the write queue. Every queued write that has not reached the database is stored, also one whose request was out or that was waiting for a retry when the app was killed, and it is replayed at the next launch. Changing `CACHE_BUSTER` in [src/lib/persistence.js](src/lib/persistence.js) — the way to discard cached reads whose shape changed — or leaving the app closed for more than 14 days drops the cached reads and keeps the queue (and a draft that is ahead of the server). Because of that, a mutation key of [src/lib/offlineMutations.js](src/lib/offlineMutations.js) is never removed or renamed; a write whose variables change shape gets a new key.
