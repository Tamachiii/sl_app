## docs/INVARIANTS.md — Web Push (rest-timer "Rest done" lock-screen notification)

Replace the bullet that starts "**Custom service worker, not generated.**" — with the sentence `pwa-update-flow.md` adds at its end ("Its `install`, `activate` and `message` listeners are the update flow's: three lines…") — with these five bullets:

- **Custom service worker, not generated.** `vite-plugin-pwa` runs in `injectManifest` mode against [src/sw.js](../src/sw.js); the generated SW is gone. The custom SW still precaches the app shell + Google Fonts via workbox (so offline behaviour is unchanged) and listens to six events. Five of them are one line each, which calls a function of [src/lib/swHandlers.js](../src/lib/swHandlers.js) with the worker's scope: `push` → `handlePush`, `notificationclick` → `handleNotificationClick`, and `install`, `activate`, `message` → the update flow's three (see "App updates"). What the worker does is in that file, where vitest runs it (`src/lib/swHandlers.test.js`); `src/sw.test.js` loads the real `sw.js` and holds the wiring. `pushsubscriptionchange` is still written in `sw.js` itself. Workbox is pulled in transitively by `vite-plugin-pwa`; don't add it to `package.json` explicitly.
- **Every push shows a notification. Never skip `showNotification`, for any reason — an open app included.** A push whose handler shows nothing is a silent push, which a subscription made with `userVisibleOnly` does not allow: WebKit counts them and takes the subscription away, and the device gets no notification at all until its owner turns them on again. So `handlePush` has no early return, and three things in it exist only for this rule: `readPushPayload` never throws (text that is not JSON becomes the body, JSON that is not an object and a payload that cannot be read are still shown, under the name of the app); the tag is never empty (`renotify` without a tag is a `TypeError`, and a `showNotification` that throws has shown nothing); and the one question asked before the notification is shown — is the app in front? — is asked for the tag `REST_TIMER_TAG` only and has `IN_FRONT_ANSWER_MS` to be answered, after which the push is shown the usual way. A condition in front of `handlePush` in `sw.js` breaks the rule just as well; `src/sw.test.js` shows a "Rest done" to a focused window for that reason.
- **"Rest done" in front of the athlete is shown quietly and taken away — the tag `rest-timer` is what says which push that is.** The page that runs a rest beeps and vibrates at its end by itself ([hooks/useRestTimerEffects.js](../src/hooks/useRestTimerEffects.js)), so the system banner of the push is a second cue for the same thing. When a push tagged `REST_TIMER_TAG` arrives while a window of the app is focused and not hidden (`appIsInFront`), `handlePush` shows it with `notificationFor(payload, { quiet: true })` — `silent: true`, `renotify: false` and **no `vibrate` key at all**: `silent` together with `vibrate` is a `TypeError` — and closes it `FOCUSED_REST_CLOSE_MS` later (`closeNotifications`, by tag). `silent` is a request a browser may ignore; the close is the part that does not depend on it. A browser that refuses the quiet form gets the usual one, which stays. Every other tag is shown as usual whatever the app is doing, and so is a "Rest done" with the app in the background, with no window, or with a window that is visible but not focused. **Whoever writes the payload of the rest push keeps the tag `rest-timer`** ([hooks/useRestTimerPush.js](../src/hooks/useRestTimerPush.js)): under another tag the push is an ordinary one — two cues in front of the athlete, and a notification nothing closes. "In front" relies on what makes the push arrive there at all: the page that schedules it is the page that beeps, and it takes the push back when it is left.
- **A tap brings the app to the front and moves a window only when it is somewhere else.** `handleNotificationClick` reads the address of the payload against the scope of the registration (`targetOf` — absolute, and `null` for a payload without `data.url`, with one that cannot be read, or with an address outside the app) and compares it with what each window reports through `isSameRoute`: same origin, same path, same route in the hash, where a trailing `/` does not count and `#/`, `#` and no hash are one route. The two are never compared as text — a window reports `https://host/sl_app/#/…` and a payload carries `/sl_app/#/…`, which never match, and every tap then loads the page again under its user. A window already on the page is focused and left as it is, and is preferred to any other; a window elsewhere is focused, then sent there with `navigate` (an error there is ignored: the focus is the part that is guaranteed); a window that cannot be focused is passed over for the next; with none, `openWindow` opens the address, or the app itself when the payload names none. A payload without an address focuses and moves nothing. **The payload decides where a tap leads: a "Rest done" whose `data.url` is the training list sends a window that is on the session to the list.** The session's own route in `data.url` is what leaves the athlete in the session. **Only windows of the app count** (`appWindows`: `client.url` starts with `registration.scope`): `matchAll({ includeUncontrolled: true })` lists every window of the origin, and `<account>.github.io` is shared with whatever else is published there — a tab of another site is neither "the app in front" nor a window to send somewhere. The window is asked for first and nothing is awaited before it: a worker may focus or open a window only for a short while after the tap.
- **A "Rest done" that is no longer news is closed when the app comes back — by the worker on a tap, by the page otherwise.** The tag makes the next rest replace the notification of the one before; nothing replaces the last one of a session. A tap on any notification closes the ones tagged `REST_TIMER_TAG` (`handleNotificationClick`). The worker hears nothing when the app is opened from its icon or from the app switcher, so the page does it: [src/lib/restNotifications.js](../src/lib/restNotifications.js) — `restNotifications.install()` in [src/main.jsx](../src/main.jsx), production builds only — calls `closeRestNotifications` once when it is installed on a visible page, at each `visibilitychange` to visible and at each `focus` of the window (a desktop window that stayed visible only gets the second). It asks nothing where `Notification.permission` is not `granted`, and uses `getRegistration()`, never `navigator.serviceWorker.ready`, which does not answer on a page no worker is registered for.

## README.md — Rest Timer (in-app + Web Push)

Replace "The custom service worker ([src/sw.js](src/sw.js)) renders the notification." with:

The custom service worker ([src/sw.js](src/sw.js), handlers in [src/lib/swHandlers.js](src/lib/swHandlers.js)) shows the notification — always: with the app in front of the athlete, where the in-app cue already plays, it is shown without sound and closed a few seconds later, never skipped (a push that shows nothing costs an iPhone its subscription). A tap brings the app to the front and leaves a window alone that is already on the page the notification names; a "Rest done" still on the lock screen is closed when the app is opened again.
